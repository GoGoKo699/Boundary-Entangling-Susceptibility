# Color and reading guide

## Irises as a color reference

The reference is the present appearance of Vincent van Gogh's [*Irises*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irises_(painting)), not an attempt to reconstruct its original pigments. Iris blue and leaf green form the cool core; earth tones provide a restrained contrast. The painting is not used as a background or a metaphor for the physics.

## Keep the scientific encodings intact

The preview changes colors only. Primary and independent runs keep their filled-circle and open-square markers. Solid and dashed lines remain distinct. The matrices retain their numeric signs, zero-centered normalization, axis order, and existing color-reversal convention. Matrix-label colors are selected for contrast against their actual cell backgrounds; their text, size, and position are unchanged. Figures remain on white, including in the site's dark reading mode.

In Figure 2, the response and probability-slope matrices use opposite color directions in the accepted design. The preview preserves that convention: warm tones link the low-response sector to increasing probability, not a universal negative numerical sign. The signed numbers and each panel's meaning must remain explicit.

The ochre and sage accents are not used for small text on white. Numerical labels, marker shapes, and line styles carry information that color alone must not carry. The contrast checks are a limited design test, not a complete accessibility certification.

## Reading without a manuscript

Each result page should let the reader identify the question, measured quantity, observation, and inference boundary, then reach the data table, plotting code, method, and proof. The current scientific Markdown remains the source; the website does not maintain a second rewritten copy of its claims.

The dialogue is split at its existing question headings for navigation. The full document remains available. Supporting and historical pages are accessible without filling the main reading route with checkpoint terminology.

The newly written landing and resource pages use the supplied scientific context. Style changes do not authorize changing a sign, quantifier, conditional population, uncertainty definition, or attribution. Equations appear after the notation needed to read them, and substantive limitations stay next to the conclusions they qualify.

## Preview and deployment

This is a private, locally viewable preview. It has no analytics, remote font requests, or runtime content fetches. Mathematical notation is compiled to native MathML during the build. Site search runs on a local index.

No GitHub Pages deployment is enabled. A private repository does not by itself guarantee a private published site; hosting visibility and the prepublication license status must be decided separately before publication.
